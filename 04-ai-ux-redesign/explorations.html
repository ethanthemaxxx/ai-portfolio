Confidence layer — three directions

The two directions I discarded for the confidence system, drawn out next to the one that shipped in the prototype. The discarded directions are drawn as a competent team would ship them, not as straw men — the argument against the score has to beat the good version of the score, or it has proven nothing.

the test — identical for all three

The same run, containing the same sentence: “Volcán Dark ships from Cooperativa Tolima in 14 days.” Fluent, specific, and false — the supplier record the run read says 28. The model was not hedging; there is no signal inside its output that marks this sentence as different from the correct ones around it.

One question, asked of each direction: does it give the operator a reason to catch this?

A · Message-level confidence score discarded
Reorder draft run r_4d91c7
High 87% Based on model certainty across 4 claims · source coverage 4/4

Sereno Decaf has no sales history, so I ordered the supplier minimum of 30 kg. Volcán Dark ships from Cooperativa Tolima in 14 days, so the 120 kg lands before you run dry on day 17.

NO

Worse than no — the false sentence sits inside the 87%. The score endorses the exact claim it exists to flag.

why it died

This is the good version: calibrated colour ramp, a real denominator in the subtext, an honest-looking bar. It still fails on three counts, any one of which is disqualifying. The number is uncalibrated — it comes from a model with no reliable access to its own reliability, so 87 and 62 do not mean what the reader thinks they mean. It is message-level, so it dilutes: three solid facts and one invention average to something comfortable, and the invention is in there, invisible. And it is unactionable — nobody has ever known what to do differently at 87 versus 62.

It survives in products because it is trivial to render and looks like rigour. That is a reason to ship it, not a reason it works.

B · The persistent disclaimer discarded

i AI-generated. May contain mistakes — verify important information. Learn more
NO

The warning on this run is identical to the warning on a perfect run. A warning that never varies carries no information.

why it died

Again the good version: small, quiet, correctly worded, exactly as the major assistants ship it — and shipped for defensible reasons, because at zero cost it is legally prudent and never overclaims. But it is on every screen, so it is read on none. It is liability text wearing an interface’s clothes: it transfers responsibility to the operator without giving them one specific thing to check.

The disqualifying property is that it is constant. The run that is fine and the run with the false lead time carry the same sentence, so the sentence distinguishes nothing — which is the one job a confidence layer has.

FLAGGED

The claim is checked against the record it cites, both values are shown, and the two clean items stay clean.

why it shipped

Uncertainty attaches to the claim, never to the message. Every value is one of three things — verified, from a record; derived, computed from verified numbers; assumed, a gap the model filled — and only the states that need a human get colour. The false claim is caught not by asking the model how it feels but by a deterministic check that re-reads the record the run read.

The cost, named: provenance has to be plumbed. The runtime must tag output fields with the tool result they came from, which is real engineering and assumes structured output. That price is why teams ship A and B instead — and it is the whole difference.
