# Freshness, Roast Dates & Storage

## Reading the bag

Every bag is stamped with a **roast date**, not a "best before" date. That's
deliberate — a best-before date tells you when coffee becomes undrinkable, which is
months away and useless. The roast date tells you where you are in the window where it
actually tastes good.

## The freshness window

| Days off roast | What's happening |
|---|---|
| 0–3 days | Still degassing. Filter is fine; espresso will gush and taste thin. |
| **4–21 days** | **The window.** This is where the coffee is at its best. |
| 21–35 days | Still good, gradually flattening. Aromatics fade first. |
| 35+ days | Drinkable, dull. Sweetness drops off, papery notes come in. |

Because we roast to order, coffee typically reaches you **2–5 days off roast**. If
you're pulling espresso, let it rest another 2–3 days before dialing in.

## Storage

**Do:** keep the bag sealed, at room temperature, out of direct sun. Our bags have a
one-way valve and a resealable strip — the bag it arrived in is the best container you
own. Press the air out before resealing.

**Don't:** store coffee in the fridge. It's humid and coffee absorbs odors — you'll get
notes of last week's leftovers.

**Freezing** works, but only if you do it properly: freeze in small, fully sealed,
single-dose portions, and never refreeze a portion once it's thawed. Condensation is
what ruins frozen coffee. For a bag you'll finish in three weeks, freezing is not worth
the trouble.

## Whole bean vs. ground

Ground coffee stales roughly **an order of magnitude faster** than whole bean —
surface area is the enemy. Whole bean holds its window for about three weeks;
pre-ground is noticeably flatter after four or five days.

If you don't have a grinder, buy smaller bags more often rather than a large bag you'll
work through slowly. A 250g bag every two weeks beats a 1kg bag every eight.

## Common freshness complaints

**"It tastes sour."** Usually under-extraction, not staleness. Grind finer or brew
hotter before you blame the beans.

**"It tastes flat and papery."** That's age. Check the roast date on the bag — if it's
more than 14 days before you received it, that's on us, and the returns policy covers
you.

**"There's no crema on my espresso."** Coffee that's too fresh (under 4 days) or too old
(over 5 weeks) both cause this. Check the roast date first.

**"The bag is puffed up."** That's CO₂ from a very fresh roast pushing through the
valve. It's a good sign.
